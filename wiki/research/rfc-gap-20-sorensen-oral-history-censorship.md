# RFC GAP-20: Sorensen 1953 Oral History Archival Delta

> **Executive Summary:** In 1953, Charles E. Sorensen completed an unvarnished, 1,000+ page oral history interview with Dr. Owen Bombard for the Ford Motor Company Archives (BFRC Accession 65). Co-author and editor Samuel T. Williamson substantially restructured, polished, and sanitized this raw transcript into the commercial 1956 publication *My Forty Years with Ford*. Cross-textual archival comparison isolates critical deletions regarding Henry Ford's cognitive decline, Harry Bennett's underworld connections, and specific shop-floor engineering rivalries.

---

## 1. Archival Comparison Matrix

| Thematic Category | Raw 1953 Oral History (BFRC Acc. 65, Boxes 66–69) | 1956 Published Commercial Memoir (*My Forty Years with Ford*) | Archival Effect / Censorship Motive |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Henry Ford Mental Deterioration** | Detailed accounts of post-1938 stroke-induced erratic behavior, memory lapses, and paranoid delusions regarding family betrayal. | Softened into tragic eccentricities, maintaining Ford's industrial genius while portraying his later years as sadly manipulated by Bennett. | Mitigated potential libel claims from surviving Ford family members and preserved book marketability. |
| **Harry Bennett & Underworld Ties** | Explicit references to Bennett's direct relationships with Detroit underworld figures (Chester LaMare mob), armed plant intimidation, and illegal wiretapping of executive offices. | Condensed into generic descriptions of "Service Department excesses" and palace intrigue. | Avoided legal exposure and protected corporate reputation during the 1956 FMC Initial Public Offering (IPO). |
| **Shop-Floor Credit Erasure** | Extensive, casual mentions of foreman-level mechanics (Klann, Avery, Galamb, Farkas, Martin) and their day-to-day trial modifications. | Re-centered narrative focus on Sorensen as the central architect of mass production and Willow Run layout. | Commercial narrative pacing and ghostwriter biographical simplification. |
| **The Ouster Negotiations (1944)** | Frank descriptions of the cold boardroom ultimatum delivered to Sorensen following Edsel's death. | Framed as a dignified, voluntary retirement driven by wartime physical exhaustion. | Preserved Sorensen's executive stature and historical legacy. |
