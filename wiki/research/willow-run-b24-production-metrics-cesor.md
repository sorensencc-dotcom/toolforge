# Willow Run B-24 Production Metrics & CESOR Industrial Role Dossier

> Canonical research node synthesising Charles E. Sorensen's (CESOR) role in scaling Willow Run B-24 bomber production, including primary source testimony from *My Forty Years With Ford*, verified output metrics, and treatment beats for documentary production.

## 1. CESOR Industrial Role & Conception Matrix

| # | Claim | Verification Status | Evidentiary Grounding | Primary & Archival Citation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Coronado Hotel Overnight Sketch** | **VERIFIED** | In Jan 1941, Sorensen, Edsel Ford, Henry Ford II, and Benson Ford toured Consolidated Aircraft's San Diego plant. Observing craft-style outdoor assembly designed for 1 aircraft/day, Sorensen sketched a continuous-flow mass-production facility overnight at the Coronado Hotel on hotel stationery. Edsel Ford and his sons initialed the sketch the following morning. | Charles E. Sorensen, *My Forty Years With Ford*, pp. 280–288; lines 28240–28374; 28609–28621. |
| 2 | **"Complete Plane or Nothing" Contract Ultimatum** | **VERIFIED** | Consolidated offered Ford a subcontract to build 1,000 wing sets. Sorensen rejected component-only subcontracts, stating "We'll make the complete plane or nothing," proposing to build complete B-24 bombers at a rate of 1 per hour (540/month) backed by up to \$200M in federal plant investment. | *My Forty Years With Ford*, lines 28687–28710, 28750–28759; Assembly Magazine. |
| 3 | **Automotive Mass-Production Adaptation** | **VERIFIED** | Willow Run translated automotive methods (standardized stamped parts, parallel sub-assemblies, 136 mechanized conveyors) to heavy aviation airframes, replacing hand-fitting with precision interchangeable tooling. | Historic American Engineering Record (HAER); Assembly Magazine (2020). |
| 4 | **Single-Manager Authority Transition** | **INFERRED** | Initial production suffered severe delays from ongoing Air Corps design modifications, divided authority, and tooling bottlenecks. Later consolidation under single-manager automotive command stabilized line cadence. | Army Air Forces Historical Studies; Willow Run plant histories. |

## 2. Production Metrics & Deliveries Table

| Metric Category | Verified Figure | Status | Archival Source & Corroboration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Completed Flyaway Bombers** | **6,792 aircraft** | **VERIFIED** | Models B-24E, H, J, L, M, and N accepted off the Willow Run final line. Steven Puhl (Willow Run historian); Kassel Mission production pool; AAF Statistical Digest. |
| **Knock-Down (KD) Kits** | **1,893 units** | **VERIFIED** | Shipped via specialized 60-ft highway truck-trailers to Douglas (Tulsa, OK: 962 kits) and Consolidated (Fort Worth, TX: 931 kits). |
| **Combined Airframe Output** | **8,685 airframes** | **VERIFIED** | Aligns with Charles E. Sorensen's published claim of ~8,800 Liberators within standard rounding and spares accounting. |
| **Peak Line Assembly Cadence** | **1 B-24 every 63 working minutes** | **VERIFIED** | Achieved spring 1944 across two 9-hour shifts with restocking windows; not a continuous 24-hour calendar clock. |
| **Peak Monthly Acceptance** | **428–500 aircraft / month** | **INFERRED** | Monthly high achieved in mid-1944 before War Production Board cutbacks due to aircraft surplus. |
| **Final Assembly Rollout** | **28 June 1945** | **VERIFIED** | Production terminated with B-24M-30-FO (serial 44-51928), christened *The Campbell*. |
| **Plant Employment** | **~42,000 peak** | **INFERRED** | Rose to 42,000 mid-war before declining to ~17,000 as line learning curves and efficiency matured. |

## 3. Documentary Treatment Beats ("The Biggest Challenge")

- **Scene 1 (San Diego, Jan 1941)**: Visual juxtaposition of Southern California outdoor craft assembly with Sorensen's Detroit mass-production perspective.
- **Scene 2 (Coronado Hotel Overnight)**: Sorensen breaking the 30,000-part Liberator into manageable modular sub-assemblies on hotel notepad; Edsel Ford and sons initialing the blueprint at breakfast.
- **Scene 3 (The Washington Showdown)**: "The complete plane or nothing." Ford commitments to 1 bomber per hour.
- **Scene 4 (The 63-Minute Miracle)**: The assembly line reaching peak velocity in spring 1944, producing airframe weight rivaling national industrial outputs.
