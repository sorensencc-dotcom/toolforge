# Dodge Brothers vs. Henry Ford (GAP-04)

> Legal and corporate governance dossier on *Dodge v. Ford Motor Co.* (204 Mich. 459, 170 N.W. 668, 1919), shareholder primacy, dividend withholding, and the capitalization of the River Rouge complex.

## Historical & Legal Context

In **1916**, John and Horace Dodge (holding a 10% minority equity stake in Ford Motor Company) sued Henry Ford over his refusal to distribute surplus capital as special dividends:
1. **The Conflict of Intentions**:
   * Henry Ford declared an explicit corporate policy of reinvesting accumulated earnings (over \$58 million surplus) to expand production capacity, lower vehicle prices for the public, and construct the massive, vertically integrated **River Rouge complex**.
   * The Dodge brothers sought dividend capital to finance their competing automotive firm (**Dodge Brothers Motor Vehicle Company**, founded 1914).
2. **The Landmark 1919 Ruling**:
   * The **Michigan Supreme Court** ruled in favor of the Dodge brothers on dividends, establishing the doctrine of **shareholder primacy**: *"A business corporation is organized and carried on primarily for the profit of the stockholders."*
   * The court ordered the distribution of a **\$19.3 million special dividend** plus statutory interest.
   * Crucially, the court affirmed the **Business Judgment Rule** regarding long-term manufacturing expansion, refusing to enjoin the purchase of land and blast furnaces for the River Rouge plant.
3. **The 1919 Buyout Climax**:
   * Following the ruling, Henry Ford threatened to leave the company and establish an independent enterprise (*Henry Ford & Son*).
   * This manufactured crisis panicked minority shareholders, allowing Edsel and Henry Ford to buy out all minority equity (including the Dodge brothers' stake for **\$25 million**) in July 1919, concentrating 100% ownership within the Ford family until the 1956 public offering.
