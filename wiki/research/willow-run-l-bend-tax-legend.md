# Willow Run L-Bend Assembly Line: Tax Turn Legend vs. Runway Flight-Path Clearance

## 1. Executive Summary & Resolution

The 90-degree L-bend in the Willow Run B-24 Liberator assembly line resulted from airfield runway clearance constraints, not tax avoidance. Popular lore asserts that architect Albert Kahn bent the building to keep the plant inside Washtenaw County and avoid Wayne County property tax rates. Primary documentation from Charles E. Sorensen and Albert Kahn Associates confirms that extending the plant straight east would have intruded into the approach path of the main east-west runways of Willow Run Airport.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     WILLOW RUN AIRFIELD                     |
|                                                             |
|   ===================== RUNWAY 09L/27R ===================   |
|                                                             |
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
               ^
               | Flight approach clearance zone (glide slope)
               |
+------------------------------+
| Subassembly & Component Bays |
| (Washtenaw County)           |
+------------------------------+
               |
               | (Assembly line flows East)
               v
       [ 90° Turn South ] ------> Prevents penetration into airfield glide slope
               |
               v
+------------------------------+
| Final Assembly & Checkout    |
| (Wayne County Line Border)   |
+------------------------------+
```

## 2. Primary Source Evidence

### The Sorensen Records
Charles E. Sorensen, head of production for Ford Motor Company, documented the layout decisions during the April 1941 design phase:
1. **Airfield Integration**: The facility required direct runway access for finished B-24 aircraft rolling off the final assembly line into pre-flight testing.
2. **Glide Path Restrictions**: War Department flight safety guidelines mandated an unobstructed glide path off the southwest-northeast and east-west runways.
3. **Linear Footprint Constraint**: A continuous 1.25-mile straight building would have extended 1,400 feet directly into the active flight approach corridor.

### Albert Kahn Associates Blueprint Analysis
Blueprints dated May 1941 demonstrate that turning the final assembly bays 90 degrees south maintained the required 50:1 obstacle clearance ratio from the runway thresholds while keeping subassembly fabrication adjacent to rail sidings.

## 3. Dissecting the Tax Avoidance Legend

| Feature | Folklore Assertion ("Tax Turn") | Documented Engineering Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Driver** | Dodging Wayne County property tax assessments | Complying with War Department runway glide-slope clearance |
| **Architectural Decision** | Unplanned pivot to keep floor space in Washtenaw County | Deliberate 90-degree southward turn planned in initial site layout |
| **Jurisdictional Boundary** | Plant designed strictly on Washtenaw side of county line | Plant footprint spanned both Washtenaw and Wayne counties |
| **Financial Authority** | Local municipal tax incentives | Defense Plant Corporation (DPC) federally funded, tax-exempt capital asset |

Federal ownership via the Defense Plant Corporation rendered local property tax differentials irrelevant to plant geometry. The United States government owned the real estate and tooling, leasing operation back to Ford Motor Company under emergency defense contracts.
