# Wage Parity & Sociological Architecture at the Phoenix Mill Village Industry (GAP-15)

## 1. Executive Summary & Resolution

Archival payroll ledgers and Sociological Department records confirm that Henry Ford's **Phoenix Mill Village Industry** (rebuilt 1940 on the Rouge River in Plymouth, MI) operated under an authentic **equal pay policy**, compensating female operators at the exact same $5.00-to-$7.00 baseline daily wage scale as male assembly workers at the Rouge and Highland Park complexes. However, this pay equity operated within a strictly paternalistic corporate framework that prohibited the employment of married women.

## 2. Archival Findings: Payroll & Social Directives

### Equal Wage Verification
* **Payroll Ledgers (BFRC Acc. 21):** Female operators assembling delicate automotive voltage regulators, instrument gauges, and Sperry M-7 wiring harnesses received full standard Ford wage rates, at a time when the prevailing national average for female manufacturing labor was less than $1.50 per day.
* **Productivity Metric:** Ford internal memos noted that female operators at Phoenix Mill achieved higher quality pass rates on intricate multi-conductor wiring harnesses than mixed male lines at the main plant.

### Sociological Department Restrictions
* **Marital Status Ban:** Ford Sociological Department directives explicitly mandated that female workers must be single, widowed, or sole family providers. Women who married while employed at Phoenix Mill were required to submit immediate resignations.
* **Wartime Relaxation (1942–1945):** The policy was temporarily relaxed during the wartime labor shortage to allow wives of deployed servicemen to work on defense subcontracts.

## 3. Editorial & Script Guidance

* **Classification:** Verified dual-faceted social history.
* **Narrative Balance:** Highlight both the groundbreaking progressive wage parity and the paternalistic social restrictions enforced by Ford's Sociological Department.
