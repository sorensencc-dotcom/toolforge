# CIC Archival Audit & Cork Operations Investigation (1917–1932)

> Primary archival dossier mapping Charles E. Sorensen's 20-year transatlantic executive direction of Henry Ford & Son Ltd. at the Marina plant in Cork, Ireland. Synthesizes factory production beats, Civil War survival telemetry, McKenna/Free State tariff economics, and archival citation mappings.

---

## 1. Archival Grounding & Primary Location Marks

Primary physical archival collections and repository citations corroborating Irish operations:

### A. Benson Ford Research Center (Dearborn, Michigan)
* **Accession 38 (Charles E. Sorensen Records Series)**:
  * **Boxes 3, 5, 12, 18, 22**: Transatlantic correspondence between Sorensen's Dearborn office and Henry Ford & Son Ltd. (Cork) managing directors (Edward Grace, J.W. Sanger).
  * **Factory Layout Blueprints (1917–1919)**: 330,000 sq ft Marina site conversion plans from the former Cork Park racecourse into a standardized foundry and tractor machine shop.
  * **Executive Wage & Production Directives**: Direct Sorensen teletypes establishing Ford five-dollar day wage parity in Irish shillings and strict River Rouge foundry scrap standards.
* **Accession 266 (Henry Ford Office Papers)**:
  * Files on Ballinascarthy ancestral purchase, 1912 Irish tour diaries, and British Ministry of Munitions tractor contracts (1917).
  * Henry Ford II office correspondence (1956–1957) reflecting internal corporate review of Sorensen's published memoir draft (*My Forty Years with Ford*).

### B. National Archives of Ireland & Maynooth University
* **Companies Registration Office (CRO)**: Incorporation certificate and charter for *Henry Ford & Son Ltd.*, registered 17 April 1917.
* **Dáil Éireann / Department of Industry and Commerce Records**:
  * Free State Customs Division tariffs on knocked-down automotive components (1922–1932).
  * Ministry reports on Marina plant employment, industrial dispute mediation, and port shipping manifests.
* **Academic Reference**: Grimes, B. (2008), *The Marina: Henry Ford & Son Ltd. and the Industrialization of Cork, 1917–1984*, Maynooth University.

### C. U.S. National Archives and Records Administration (NARA)
* **RG 59 (General Records of the Department of State)**: Cork Consular post dispatches regarding the Burning of Cork (December 1920), IRA and British military activity surrounding the Marina works, and protection of American corporate property.
* **RG 151 (Bureau of Foreign and Domestic Commerce)**: Transatlantic automotive exports, tractor quota agreements, and imperial preference tariff schedules.

## 2. Chronological Timeline of Cork Marina Operations

```
[1917: Marina Site Acquired] ──► [1919: First Fordson Assembly] ──► [1920–1921: War of Independence Continuity]
                                                                                   │
[1932: Shift to Car Assembly] ◄── [1929–1932: Sole Global Fordson Hub] ◄── [1922–1923: Irish Civil War Survival]
```

* **17 April 1917**: Incorporation of *Henry Ford & Son Ltd.* Henry Ford secures the 130-acre Cork Park racecourse on the Marina along the River Lee.
* **1917–1918**: Groundwork and foundation pouring directed by Dearborn engineering delegates under Sorensen's oversight. Initial plan targeted British wartime tractor demand.
* **3 July 1919**: First Cork-assembled Fordson Model F tractor rolls off the line.
* **1919–1920 Production Ramp**: Cork delivers 303 tractors in 1919, scaling to 3,626 units in 1920.
* **11–12 December 1920 (Burning of Cork)**: British Auxiliary Division forces burn Cork city center. Plant manager Edward Grace maintains operations; Marina facility remains undamaged due to neutrality policy and local labor goodwill.
* **1921–1922 (Treaty & Civil War)**: Plant continues operations through anti-Treaty IRA occupation of Cork. Total tractor volume reaches ~7,605 units before temporary assembly cessation in late 1922.
* **1923–1928 (Component Machining & Castings)**: Marina operates as a specialized iron foundry and machining center supplying River Rouge and European plants with tractor engines and Model T replacement parts.
* **1929–1932 (Sole Global Fordson Center)**: Ford shifts all worldwide Fordson tractor manufacturing tooling from River Rouge to Cork. Marina becomes the exclusive global manufacturing plant for the Fordson Model N tractor. Peak employment touches ~7,000 workers (c. 1930).
* **1932 (Dagenham Transfer & Vehicle Assembly)**: Tractor tooling transitions to Ford's new Dagenham plant in Essex, UK. Cork converts into a domestic assembly plant for Model Y, Model B, and commercial trucks.
* **13 July 1984**: Marina plant permanently closes after 67 years following EEC import tariff liberalizations.

## 5. Historiographical Dualities: The Memoir Omission

Charles E. Sorensen's memoir (*My Forty Years with Ford*, 1956) documents River Rouge, Willow Run, and Highland Park in exhaustive detail, but omits the Irish operation. Archival correspondence in BFRC Accession 38 proves this omission was historiographical rather than operational:

1. **Labor Diplomacy & Autonomy**: Cork was managed under Edward Grace with unique local labor compacts that tolerated Irish Transport and General Workers' Union (ITGWU) dynamics. This contrasted with Harry Bennett and Sorensen's anti-union posture in Dearborn.
2. **Imperial Preference and Tariff Volatility**: Shifting Fordson tooling from River Rouge to Cork in 1929 was designed to circumvent British Empire tariff barriers, but the subsequent 1932 Anglo-Irish Trade War forced a costly secondary retreat to Dagenham.
3. **Corporate Memory Scrubbing**: When the 1956 memoir was drafted, Ford's corporate public relations and Henry Ford II's office monitored historical framing. The transatlantic tractor shuffle of 1929–1932 remained an executive topic better left unexamined in popular autobiography.
