# Harry Bennett's Service Department Authority (GAP-05)

> Archival dossier investigating the shadow authority, plant surveillance mechanisms, and executive conflict generated by Harry Bennett and the Ford Service Department at the River Rouge and Willow Run plants.

## Historical & Organizational Dynamics

From the late 1920s until September 1945, **Harry Herbert Bennett** operated the **Ford Service Department** as a private internal security and intelligence apparatus directly answerable only to an aging Henry Ford:
1. **Scope and Paramilitary Structure**:
   * Numbered approximately **3,000 agents, ex-boxers, underworld figures, and plant guards**.
   * Controlled access gates, timekeeping checkpoints, plant communications, and personnel surveillance across the 1,200-acre River Rouge complex and later sections of the Willow Run bomber plant.
2. **Executive Bypassing & Subversion**:
   * **Edsel Ford (President)**: Bennett systematically undermined Edsel's administrative initiatives, vetoed progressive labor policies, and monitored Edsel's executive telephone lines and mail.
   * **Charles E. Sorensen (Production Head)**: Bennett maintained an uneasy truce with Sorensen until the 1940s, eventually encroaching on manufacturing authority by appointing loyal plant superintendents (e.g., Ray Rausch) and manufacturing procurement contacts.
   * **A.M. Wibel (Director of Purchasing)**: Wibel resigned in 1943 after Bennett forced through unvetted supplier contracts with underworld-linked vendors.
3. **Declassified Security & Labor Records**:
   * **The Battle of the Overpass (May 26, 1937)**: Service Department men violently assaulted UAW organizers (Walter Reuther, Richard Frankensteen) on Bridge 4 outside Gate 4, resulting in landmark NLRB sanctions (*NLRB v. Ford Motor Co.*, 114 F.2d 905).
   * **Willow Run Infiltration (1942–1944)**: Bennett attempted to place Service Department personnel in charge of airfield gate security and worker vetting, leading to direct pushback from Army Air Forces military intelligence and the War Department.
4. **The Purge (September 21, 1945)**:
   * Immediately upon assuming the presidency from Henry Ford, **Henry Ford II** confronted Bennett in the basement office at the Rouge and fired him, disbanding the Service Department on the same day.

## Authority & Operational Breakdown

| Sphere of Influence | Administrative Mechanism | Key Affected Executive | Primary Conflict / Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plant Security & Intelligence** | 3,000-man Service Department; undercover plant spotters | Edsel Ford / Plant Superintendents | Total surveillance of plant floors; violent anti-union suppression |
| **Vendor & Supplier Contracts** | Shadow procurement channels bypassing Purchasing | A.M. Wibel (Purchasing Chief) | Resignation of Wibel in 1943 over corrupt parts contracts |
| **Manufacturing Oversight** | Alliance with Ray Rausch; encroachment on Rouge operations | Charles E. Sorensen | Sorensen's marginalization and resignation in March 1944 |
| **Corporate Termination** | Direct executive firing by Henry Ford II (Sept 21, 1945) | Harry Bennett | Total dissolution of the Service Department apparatus |
