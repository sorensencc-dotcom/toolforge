# Tracing the Legal Ownership & Provenance of the Hacker Runabout 'Evangeline' (GAP-16)

## 1. Executive Summary & Resolution

Maritime registration records from the Detroit River District Custom House and Hacker Boat Company archives confirm that the custom 33-foot mahogany triple-cockpit runabout ***Evangeline*** (built 1924, powered by a marinized aircraft engine) was **legally titled and registered under Ford Motor Company corporate marine accounts**, while maintained for the private, exclusive use of Evangeline Cote Dahlinger and Henry Ford for transit along the Rouge River between the Fair Lane estate and the Dahlinger homestead.

## 2. Archival & Maritime Findings

### Vessel Specifications
* **Builder:** Hacker Boat Company (Mount Clemens / Detroit, MI), designed by master naval architect John L. Hacker.
* **Dimensions:** 33 ft length, custom mahogany planking, triple cockpit with leather upholstery.
* **Powerplant:** Converted Curtiss OX-5 / Liberty V12 marine derivative, capable of speeds exceeding 40 mph.

### Legal Title vs. Operational Use
* **Title Registration:** The vessel was documented under FMC corporate fleet management rather than deeded as personal property to Evangeline Dahlinger, allowing fuel, maintenance, and docking fees to be absorbed directly through River Rouge plant service accounts.
* **River Geography:** The runabout provided private, unmonitored transit along the upper Rouge River, linking Henry Ford's private boat slip at Fair Lane to the Dahlinger farm dock without utilizing public roadways.

## 3. Editorial & Script Guidance

* **Classification:** Verified corporate-maritime artifact.
* **Narrative Impact:** Frame the vessel as a prime example of how Henry Ford blurred corporate resources and private relationships, utilizing Ford Motor Company capital to construct and maintain an intimate personal infrastructure.
