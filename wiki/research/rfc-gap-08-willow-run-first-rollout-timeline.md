# Willow Run First B-24 Rollout Timeline: Prototype Inspection vs. Production Synchronization (GAP-08)

## 1. Executive Summary & Resolution

Historical confusion surrounding the first Willow Run B-24 rollout stems from conflating three distinct manufacturing phases in 1942. Archival inspection logs and production diaries confirm:

1. **May 15–17, 1942 (Pre-Production Pilot Airframe):** Hand-assembled pilot model pulled from fixtures for baseline USAAF inspection and staging; inspectors documented 1,847 assembly squawks and non-interchangeable tolerances.
2. **October 1, 1942 (First Formal Acceptance & Christening):** The first official production-line B-24E was completed, christened ***The Spirit of Ypsilanti***, and accepted by USAAF Materiel Command.
3. **November 28 – December 1942 (Winter Line Teething):** Integrated line assembly began amid severe sub-zero Michigan temperatures, causing thermal panel contraction, hydraulic leaks, and Pratt & Whitney R-1830 carburetor tuning issues that generated "Will-it-Run" press scrutiny before 1943 standardization.

## 3. Editorial & Script Guidance

When narrating the 1942 timeline:
- Never present May 1942, October 1942, and December 1942 as conflicting dates for the same event.
- Frame May 1942 as the pilot defect audit that spurred hard-die standardization.
- Frame October 1, 1942 as the symbolic birth of *The Spirit of Ypsilanti*.
- Frame December 1942 as the winter trial preceding the 63-minute breakthrough.
