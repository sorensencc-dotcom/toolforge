# Cuban Seizures & Retired Assets Dossier

## Historical Context & Expropriation Framework
Following the 1959 Cuban Revolution, the Cuban Revolutionary Government enacted sweeping nationalization decrees:
- **Agrarian Reform Law (May 17, 1959):** Expropriated private agricultural holdings and sugar estates exceeding statutory limits.
- **Nationalization Law 851 (July 6, 1960):** Authorized the total expropriation of properties, assets, and enterprises owned by United States nationals, subsidiaries, and corporate entities.
- **Executive Resolution Decrees (August-October 1960):** Seized major utility infrastructure (Cuban Electric Company, Cuban Telephone Company/ITT), petroleum refineries (Texaco, Shell, Standard Oil), and all U.S.-owned sugar mills and port facilities.

## Legal Precedents & Certified Claims
- **FCSC Adjudication:** Under Title V of the International Claims Settlement Act of 1949, the Foreign Claims Settlement Commission (FCSC) certified 5,911 claims totaling \$1.8 billion in principal value (\$8-9+ billion adjusted for current value and interest).
- **Act of State Doctrine & Sabbatino:** In *Banco Nacional de Cuba v. Sabbatino* (376 U.S. 398, 1964), the Supreme Court applied the Act of State doctrine, which led directly to the Congressional enactment of the **Second Hickenlooper Amendment** (22 U.S.C. § 2370(e)(2)) restricting judicial abstention in foreign expropriation claims violating international law.
- **LIBERTAD Act (Helms-Burton Act of 1996):** Codified Title III private rights of action against entities trafficking in confiscated property.
