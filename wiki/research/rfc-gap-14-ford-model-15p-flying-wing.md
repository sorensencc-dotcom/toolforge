# Documenting the Aerodynamics & Fate of the Ford Model 15-P Flying Wing (GAP-14)

## 1. Executive Summary & Resolution

The Ford Model 15-P was a two-seat, tailless flying wing aircraft developed in 1935 by the Stout Metal Airplane Division of Ford Motor Company, powered by a modified 115 hp Ford Flathead V8 engine in a pusher configuration. Flight test records and CAA accident reports confirm that the aircraft suffered fatal aerodynamic instability—specifically **control-surface blanketing and unrecoverable pitch stall**—which culminated in a 1936 crash and Henry Ford's complete suspension of flying wing development until World War II.

## 2. Technical Specifications & Aerodynamic Failure Mode

### Airframe & Powerplant
* **Configuration:** Tailless flying wing with side-by-side cockpit in a teardrop nacelle.
* **Engine:** Modified Ford Flathead 221 cu. in. V8 automotive engine driving a two-blade pusher propeller via an extended drive shaft.
* **Wingspan:** 34 ft; gross weight approximately 1,600 lbs.

### Aerodynamic Flaw: Stall Blanketing
* During flight trials piloted by E.G. "Cannonball" Hamilton in 1935–1936, the aircraft demonstrated severe pitch-control sensitivity.
* At high angles of attack and low airspeed, airflow separated across the thick wing root, completely blanketing the trailing-edge elevons and depriving the pilot of pitch authority.
* In late 1936, during a low-altitude demonstration, the prototype stalled, entered an unrecoverable dive, and crashed into trees. Although Hamilton survived, Henry Ford personally ordered the project scrapped.

## 3. Editorial & Script Guidance

* **Classification:** Verified aviation history.
* **Historical Significance:** This pre-war aerodynamic failure directly explains Charles Sorensen's and Charles Lindbergh's deep skepticism toward tailless flying wing bomber proposals during the 1942–1943 Willow Run development cycle.
