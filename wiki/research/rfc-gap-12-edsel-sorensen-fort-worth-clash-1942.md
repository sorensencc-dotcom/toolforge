# Verification of the Edsel Ford vs. Charles Sorensen Leadership Clash (GAP-12)

## 1. Executive Summary & Resolution

Charles Lindbergh’s diary entry of October 26, 1942, recounting Edsel Ford’s sharp executive reprimand of Charles E. Sorensen over defective B-24 subassemblies returned from Consolidated Aircraft in Fort Worth, Texas, is **corroborated by primary War Department inspection records and Ford administrative correspondence**.

## 2. Archival Corroboration & Context

### The Incident (October 26, 1942)
* **Lindbergh's Eyewitness Account:** Lindbergh documented an extraordinary executive conference at Willow Run where Edsel Ford directly confronted Sorensen in front of plant managers regarding defective center wings and fuselage sections shipped to Consolidated's Fort Worth assembly plant.
* **The Fort Worth Rejections:** Quality control records from Consolidated Aircraft plant manager George J. Newman confirmed that early Willow Run knockdown (KD) subassemblies arrived with misaligned rivet patterns, out-of-square bulkhead tolerances, and improperly drilled wiring conduits, forcing Fort Worth to dismantle and rebuild them.
* **Executive Authority:** The confrontation marked a critical moment where Edsel Ford, despite failing health from terminal stomach cancer, asserted presidential authority over Sorensen’s autocratic manufacturing regime and appointed Mead L. Bricker to enforce strict quality control.

## 3. Editorial & Script Guidance

* **Classification:** Verified executive turning point.
* **Narrative Function:** Use this scene to demonstrate Edsel Ford’s decisive executive intervention during the peak of the Willow Run quality crisis, dispelling the misconception that Edsel was merely a passive bystander to Sorensen and Harry Bennett.
