# Sorensen Monday Keelhauling Meetings (GAP-03)

> Archival synthesis resolving the historical nature of Charles E. Sorensen's weekly superintendents meetings at the Ford Rouge plant.

## Research Resolution

Analysis of the Ford Motor Company administrative structure and archival collections establishes:

1. **Non-Bureaucratic / Non-Stenographic Execution**:
   * As documented in *Ford Production Methods* (Hartley W. Barclay, 1936) and corroborated across Benson Ford Research Center (*BFRC*) records, Ford operating policy deliberately avoided formal committee hierarchies and stenographic minutes ("*Memorandums and correspondence are held at a minimum... There are no high-sounding job titles*").
   * Sorensen's weekly production conferences were operational command sessions rather than recorded committee meetings.

2. **Oral Lore vs. Preserved Records**:
   * The colloquial moniker *"Monday Keelhauling"* originated in oral lore among Rouge superintendents and department heads, later codified in executive memoirs (Sorensen's *My Forty Years with Ford*, 1956) and recorded in the *Ford Motor Company Reminiscences* (BFRC Accession 65).
   * Key eyewitness testimony from superintendents and executives—including W. C. Klann, Arthur A. Renner, Roscoe M. Smith, Anthony Harff, and Logan Miller (BFRC Acc. 65)—documents how Sorensen exercised direct, unsparing scrutiny over unit costs, scrap rates, and output quotas during these weekly reviews.

3. **Service Department Dynamic**:
   * Harry Bennett's Service Department operated outside formal manufacturing channels, with Bennett deriving authority directly from Henry Ford. While Bennett maintained plant surveillance, Sorensen retained exclusive command over Rouge shop-floor tooling, assembly lines, and production pacing until his departure in 1944.
