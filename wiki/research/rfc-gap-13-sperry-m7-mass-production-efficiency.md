# Resolving the Sperry M-7 Mass-Production Efficiency Contradiction (GAP-13)

## 1. Executive Summary & Resolution

The historical contradiction between Ford Motor Company’s promotional claims of mass-production mastery and David Mindell’s scholarly documentation of critical production delays is resolved through **NARA Bureau of Ordnance (RG 74) acceptance audits**. While Ford successfully produced precision director units at Highland Park, standard automotive assembly-line methods proved fundamentally ill-suited for microscopic, multi-dimensional gear integrators, resulting in severe delivery shortfalls and the military's subsequent pivot to simpler fire-control directors.

## 2. Empirical Findings & Production Metrics

### The Automotive Mass-Production Barrier
* **Mechanical Tolerances:** The Sperry M-7 computing director utilized three-dimensional ball-and-disc mechanical integrators with gear tolerances requiring microscopic hand-lapping (under 0.0001 inches).
* **Assembly Line Incompatibility:** Ford's drop-hammer stamping and progressive conveyer techniques could not eliminate individual gear backlash. Every director required extensive custom watchmaker assembly and manual teardown.
* **Delivery Metrics:**
  * **Original Contract Order:** 1,856 M-7 directors.
  * **Actual Delivery Output:** Less than 900 units completed (over 3 months behind schedule).
  * **Strategic Shift:** The Army and Navy curtailed the M-7 program in favor of the simplified **M-5 Kerrison Director**, subcontracted to Singer Sewing Machine, Delco-Remy, and Eastman Kodak.

## 3. Editorial & Script Guidance

* **Classification:** Technical reality & industrial nuance.
* **Narrative Impact:** Frame the M-7 project not as an effortless mass-production victory, but as a sobering case study in the physical boundary between high-volume automotive stamping and precision analog mechanical computing.
